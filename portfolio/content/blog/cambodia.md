Five days in December, split between Phnom Penh and Siem Reap. One half is a city
still reckoning with the 1970s; the other is the largest religious monument on
earth. They're a hard pair to put in the same week, and that's exactly why the
trip works.

## Phnom Penh

### The riverfront and the palace

I started along **Chaktomuk** and the riverside walking street, where the Mekong
and the Tonlé Sap meet, busy in the evening, full of families and food carts. The
**Royal Palace** is a few steps away, its throne hall roofline stacked in gold and
green.

![The Royal Palace's gold and green throne hall, its spires rising against a blue sky](/blog/cambodia/royal-palace-throne-hall.jpg)

Inside its walls the **Silver Pagoda**, named for the five thousand silver tiles
covering its floor, and the white stupas built for past kings around it. Dress
properly: shoulders and knees covered, or you're not getting in.

![A white stone stupa at the Silver Pagoda, the palace's gold roofline visible behind it](/blog/cambodia/silver-pagoda-stupa.jpg)

![Several ornate white and gold stupas lined up across the Silver Pagoda's courtyard](/blog/cambodia/royal-palace-stupas.jpg)

![A bronze scale model of Angkor Wat on the lawn in front of the Silver Pagoda](/blog/cambodia/silver-pagoda-angkor-model.jpg)

The **Russian Market** (Toul Tom Poung) is the one to go to for food stalls at the
back rather than souvenirs at the front, and I finished that first day at **Wat
Phnom**, the hilltop temple that gives the city its name, lit gold against the
dark, and a **sunset cruise on the Mekong**, cheap, an hour or two long, and the
best view you'll get of the city's skyline.

![A stone staircase lined with lion statues leading up to Wat Phnom, lit gold at night](/blog/cambodia/wat-phnom-at-night.jpg)

### Tuol Sleng

The next morning was **Tuol Sleng**, the former school the Khmer Rouge turned into
S-21 prison, and the **Choeung Ek killing fields** outside the city. There is no
gentle way to describe either. Take the audio guide, take your time, and don't
plan anything demanding for the rest of the day.

I walked it off at the **Olympic Stadium**, which is the opposite in every way:
a piece of 1960s modernist architecture that the whole neighbourhood uses in the
late afternoon for football, running and aerobics classes on the terraces.

### The last morning

The **National Museum**, the best collection of Khmer sculpture anywhere, and a
useful primer before Angkor, and the **Independence Monument**, before heading
north to Siem Reap.

![The National Museum's red terracotta facade, framed by a banana tree](/blog/cambodia/national-museum.jpg)

![The red sandstone Independence Monument on its roundabout, a Cambodian flag flying beside it](/blog/cambodia/independence-monument.jpg)

## Siem Reap

### Day 1, the Grand Circuit and Banteay Srei

A 9:30 start by tuk-tuk, with a driver for the whole day. The Grand Circuit is the
outer loop and it's much quieter than the famous temples: **Preah Khan**, huge and
half-collapsed.

![Preah Khan's ruined entrance towers, three collapsed prasat spires rising above the trees](/blog/cambodia/preah-khan-gopura.jpg)

![A ruined hall at Preah Khan, its carved doorway still standing among fallen stone](/blog/cambodia/preah-khan-interior.jpg)

**Neak Pean**, a shrine on an island in the middle of a square pool, reached by a
long wooden boardwalk over the water.

![A wooden boardwalk stretching out over a lily-covered pool toward the trees](/blog/cambodia/neak-pean-boardwalk.jpg)

![The Neak Pean shrine on its small island, reflected in the still pool around it](/blog/cambodia/neak-pean-shrine.jpg)

**Ta Som**, with a tree growing straight out of its east gate.

![A small tree growing out of the top of a ruined temple gate at Ta Som](/blog/cambodia/ta-som-tree-gate.jpg)

And **East Mebon** with its stone elephants at the corners.

![East Mebon's brick and laterite towers rising above its ruined outer wall](/blog/cambodia/east-mebon-towers.jpg)

Lunch was at a local place out in the countryside, and the day ended at **Pre Rup**
for the sunset, a steep pyramid temple in red laterite with a view over the whole
plain.

![The sun setting over the plain, seen from the top of Pre Rup temple](/blog/cambodia/pre-rup-sunset.jpg)

**Phnom Bakheng** is the alternative; the order is flexible, and starting
at one and finishing at the other works just as well.

### Day 2, sunrise at Angkor Wat, then the Small Circuit

A 5:00 AM pick-up, which is the single best decision of the trip.

![Angkor Wat in silhouette at sunrise, reflected in the pool in front of it](/blog/cambodia/cover.jpg)

![Angkor Wat's towers as a dark silhouette against the last blue light before dawn, reflected in the water](/blog/cambodia/angkor-wat-dawn-silhouette.jpg)

**Angkor Wat** at sunrise is crowded and still worth every minute of the alarm
clock, the towers come up black against an orange sky and the whole thing doubles
in the reflecting pool in front of it. Breakfast is at one of the restaurants just
outside the complex, after which the crowd thins out and you can walk right up to
the towers.

![Angkor Wat's three central towers seen from the approach causeway, a few visitors scattered on the terrace](/blog/cambodia/angkor-wat-approach.jpg)

![A close view of Angkor Wat's tallest tower, its carved tiers rising against a blue sky](/blog/cambodia/angkor-wat-central-tower.jpg)

Climbing to the upper level gets you a view straight down over the inner
galleries and out across the grounds.

![A view down over Angkor Wat's inner courtyards and galleries from its upper level](/blog/cambodia/angkor-wat-view-from-top.jpg)

![Angkor Wat's towers and palm trees reflected in the pool in the late morning light](/blog/cambodia/angkor-wat-reflection.jpg)

Then **Ta Prohm**, the jungle temple, where the strangler figs have grown through
the walls and are now the only thing holding some of them up.

![A massive strangler fig tree with roots engulfing the stone towers of Ta Prohm](/blog/cambodia/ta-prohm-strangler-fig.jpg)

Finally, through the **Victory Gate** into **Angkor Thom** and the **Bayon**, with
its 200-odd giant stone faces looking in every direction.

![The causeway and South Gate of Angkor Thom, lined with stone guardian statues](/blog/cambodia/angkor-thom-south-gate.jpg)

![One of the Bayon's giant stone faces carved into a tower, looking out over the temple](/blog/cambodia/bayon-face-tower.jpg)

Done by noon, which in December heat is the right call.

### In town

Evenings in Siem Reap are **Made in Cambodia Market**, local crafts, food, live
music, and much calmer than Pub Street a few blocks away.

![The neon "Pub Street" sign lit up above the bars and crowds at night](/blog/cambodia/pub-street-sign.jpg)

## Practical notes

Hire a tuk-tuk driver for a full day rather than temple by temple; it costs less
and they'll wait for you. Buy the multi-day Angkor pass, not three singles. And
put Phnom Penh first if you can, seeing the National Museum before Angkor makes
the temples legible in a way they otherwise aren't.
