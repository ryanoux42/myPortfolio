Five days in December, split between Phnom Penh and Siem Reap. One half is a city
still reckoning with the 1970s; the other is the largest religious monument on
earth. They're a hard pair to put in the same week, and that's exactly why the
trip works.

## Phnom Penh

### The riverfront and the palace

I started along **Chaktomuk** and the riverside walking street, where the Mekong
and the Tonlé Sap meet — busy in the evening, full of families and food carts.
The **Royal Palace** is a few steps away, and inside its walls the **Silver
Pagoda**, named for the five thousand silver tiles covering its floor. Dress
properly: shoulders and knees covered, or you're not getting in.

The **Russian Market** (Toul Tom Poung) is the one to go to for food stalls at the
back rather than souvenirs at the front, and I finished that first day on a
**sunset cruise on the Mekong** — cheap, an hour or two long, and the best view
you'll get of the city's skyline.

### Tuol Sleng

The next morning was **Tuol Sleng**, the former school the Khmer Rouge turned into
S-21 prison, and the **Choeung Ek killing fields** outside the city. There is no
gentle way to describe either. Take the audio guide, take your time, and don't
plan anything demanding for the rest of the day.

I walked it off at the **Olympic Stadium**, which is the opposite in every way:
a piece of 1960s modernist architecture that the whole neighbourhood uses in the
late afternoon for football, running and aerobics classes on the terraces.

### The last morning

The **National Museum** — the best collection of Khmer sculpture anywhere, and a
useful primer before Angkor — and the **Independence Monument**, before heading
north to Siem Reap.

## Siem Reap

### Day 1 — the Grand Circuit and Banteay Srei

A 9:30 start by tuk-tuk, with a driver for the whole day. The Grand Circuit is the
outer loop and it's much quieter than the famous temples: **Preah Khan**, huge and
half-collapsed; **Neak Pean**, a shrine on an island in the middle of a square
pool; **Ta Som**, with a tree growing straight out of its east gate; and **East
Mebon** with its stone elephants at the corners.

Lunch was at a local place out in the countryside, and the day ended at **Pre Rup**
for the sunset — a steep pyramid temple in red laterite with a view over the whole
plain. **Phnom Bakheng** is the alternative; the order is flexible, and starting
at one and finishing at the other works just as well.

### Day 2 — sunrise at Angkor Wat, then the Small Circuit

A 5:00 AM pick-up, which is the single best decision of the trip.

![Angkor Wat in silhouette at sunrise, reflected in the pool in front of it](/blog/cambodia/cover.jpg)

**Angkor Wat** at sunrise is crowded and still worth every minute of the alarm
clock — the towers come up black against an orange sky and the whole thing doubles
in the reflecting pool in front of it. Breakfast is at one of the restaurants just
outside the complex, after which the crowd thins out.

Then **Ta Prohm**, the jungle temple, where the strangler figs have grown through
the walls and are now the only thing holding some of them up. Finally, through the
**Victory Gate** into **Angkor Thom** and the **Bayon**, with its 200-odd giant
stone faces looking in every direction. Done by noon, which in December heat is
the right call.

### In town

Evenings in Siem Reap are **Made in Cambodia Market** — local crafts, food, live
music, and much calmer than Pub Street a few blocks away.

## Practical notes

Hire a tuk-tuk driver for a full day rather than temple by temple; it costs less
and they'll wait for you. Buy the multi-day Angkor pass, not three singles. And
put Phnom Penh first if you can — seeing the National Museum before Angkor makes
the temples legible in a way they otherwise aren't.
