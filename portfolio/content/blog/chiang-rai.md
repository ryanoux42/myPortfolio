Chiang Rai is the quiet north of Thailand: fewer crowds than Chiang Mai, temples
that look like they were drawn rather than built, and a border where three
countries meet in the middle of a river. I spent four days there, from the
Thursday evening of 11 September to Sunday 14 September, and walked most of it.

## Thursday, arriving at night

Landing in the evening left just enough time for the city centre. I started at
the **Clock Tower**, the golden one that lights up and plays music after dark,
then wandered the streets around it before ending at the **night bazaar**.

![The gold Clock Tower lit up at night in the middle of an empty roundabout](/blog/chiang-rai/clock-tower-at-night.jpg)

It's the usual mix, food stalls, souvenirs, live music at the back, but it's a
good, cheap first meal and a gentle way to get a feel for the size of the place.
Chiang Rai's centre is small; you can cross it in twenty minutes.

## Friday, the big loop

The busiest day by far, and the one worth planning around, riding between stops
in the back of a songthaew.

![The view from the back of a songthaew truck, driving past a golden elephant arch over the road](/blog/chiang-rai/songthaew-street-view.jpg)

![Wat Rong Khun, the White Temple, reflected in its pond under a blue sky](/blog/chiang-rai/cover.jpg)

**Wat Rong Khun**, the White Temple, is the postcard. It deserves the reputation:
entirely white, covered in mirrored glass that catches the sun, and much stranger
up close than the photos suggest, the bridge to the entrance crosses a field of
sculpted hands reaching up out of the ground. Go early, before the tour buses.
Even the gold building off to the side is worth a look, and it turns out to be
the restrooms.

![The bridge over the field of sculpted hands leading to the White Temple's entrance](/blog/chiang-rai/white-temple-hands-bridge.jpg)

![A golden building with an ornate bridge and bell tower, standing beside the White Temple](/blog/chiang-rai/white-temple-golden-building.jpg)

**Wat Rong Suea Ten**, the Blue Temple, is its opposite: deep indigo walls, gold
detail, and a huge white Buddha inside. It's smaller and takes fifteen minutes,
but the contrast right after the White Temple is the point.

![A huge silver-white Buddha statue seated inside the Blue Temple's indigo and gold hall](/blog/chiang-rai/blue-temple-buddha.jpg)

**Baan Dam**, the Black House museum, completes the set. It's not a temple at all,
it's the life's work of an artist, a collection of dark wooden buildings filled
with animal bones, hides and furniture made of horns. Unsettling, and the most
memorable of the three.

![The main hall of the Black House, its dark wooden roofline curving upward against the sky](/blog/chiang-rai/black-house-baan-dam.jpg)

![A framed painting inside the Black House of a pale, twisted figure against a red background](/blog/chiang-rai/baan-dam-artwork.jpg)

From there the day headed north, past cows grazing loose along the roadside, to a
**long-neck Karen village**, the **Choui Fong tea plantation** with its rows of
green hills you can walk into,

![A cow grazing in a green field beside the road, mountains and clouds behind](/blog/chiang-rai/cow-in-the-countryside.jpg)

![Rolling rows of green tea bushes at the Choui Fong plantation, hills stretching into the distance](/blog/chiang-rai/choui-fong-tea-plantation.jpg)

and finally the **Golden Triangle**, where Thailand, Laos and Myanmar meet across
the Mekong.

![A carved sign marking the Golden Triangle, framing a view of the Mekong River through its archway](/blog/chiang-rai/golden-triangle-sign.jpg)

![The wide, muddy Mekong River curving between forested hills at the Golden Triangle](/blog/chiang-rai/mekong-river-view.jpg)

The **Opium Museum** next to it explains why that particular bend of the river was
worth so much to so many people. It's a heavy subject and the museum handles it
better than the viewpoint selfie spots outside.

## Saturday, the hike to Lalitta

Slower day, better day. I walked up towards **Wat Huay Pla Kang**, the enormous
white Guan Yin statue you can see from most of the city, stopping at the smaller
temples along the path on the way up.

![A small temple with an ornate red and gold roof, a pickup truck parked outside](/blog/chiang-rai/temple-along-the-way.jpg)

![A golden bell-shaped chedi rising above a smaller temple hall, framed by trees](/blog/chiang-rai/golden-chedi-temple.jpg)

![The white Guan Yin statue at Wat Huay Pla Kang towering above a ring of white dragon statues](/blog/chiang-rai/wat-huay-pla-kang-guanyin.jpg)

![A pair of white dragon sculptures curving along the roofline at Wat Huay Pla Kang](/blog/chiang-rai/dragon-staircase-sculpture.jpg)

![The Wat Huay Pla Kang complex seen from above, its white temple hall and red-roofed pagoda side by side](/blog/chiang-rai/wat-huay-pla-kang-complex.jpg)

**Lalitta Café** sits right next to it: little huts, ponds, bridges, and paper
umbrellas and lanterns strung up through the trees, extremely staged and
honestly a good place to sit down after the climb.

![Colourful paper umbrellas and lanterns hanging from the trees at Lalitta Café](/blog/chiang-rai/lalitta-cafe-umbrellas.jpg)

The evening was the **Saturday night market** on Thanalai Road, which is a
different animal from the nightly bazaar, longer, louder, more locals, better
food. Both are worth doing; the Saturday one if you have to pick.

## Sunday, the forest, and a wrong turn

The last morning was backpacking through the forest outside town to reach a
temple, which went well until the trail stopped being a trail and **I got lost
in the forest**.

![Walking away down a paved forest road, bamboo and dense trees on both sides](/blog/chiang-rai/lost-in-the-forest.jpg)

It took a while to backtrack and find the path again, but I eventually made it
out with no drama, check the map before following a path that looks like a
shortcut.

The afternoon was the **Chiang Rai Contemporary Art Museum**, a calm place to
finish, its garden pavilions as considered as the art inside.

![A grey-roofed garden pavilion at the top of a white stone staircase](/blog/chiang-rai/garden-pavilion.jpg)

![A large abstract painting and a glazed ceramic pot on display inside the art museum](/blog/chiang-rai/contemporary-art-museum.jpg)

Then the walk to the airport, past flooded rice paddies catching the evening
light, which is doable, flat, and gives you a last hour of the countryside
before the flight home.

![A flooded rice paddy reflecting the sky, young rice shoots planted in rows](/blog/chiang-rai/rice-paddy-fields.jpg)

## What I'd keep

Three things stayed with me: the Black House, the tea plantation, and the walk up
to Lalitta. The White Temple is the reason people come, but the days with the
least planning in them were the ones I remember best.
