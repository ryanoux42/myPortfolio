Five days in Hanoi in March, which is the good window: warm enough, grey enough
that you're not walking around in 35 degrees, and the city is at its best in that
soft light. This is a place to do on foot, with frequent coffee stops.

## Day 1, the Old Quarter

The **Old Quarter** is the reason to come. Thirty-six streets, each historically
dedicated to one trade, all of them now full of scooters, plastic stools and
food. There's no efficient way to see it; you just walk. Decorations for Tết
were still up everywhere: shopfronts wrapped in paper flowers, and a giant
flower installation shaped like an open book by the lake.

![A street food stall called "Bami Bread" wrapped in woven flowers and Tet decorations](/blog/hanoi/bami-bread-tet-shopfront.jpg)

![A giant open-book decoration made entirely of red and yellow flowers, for Tet](/blog/hanoi/flower-book-tet-display.jpg)

![A wall display of red paper fans, conical hats and glittering "26" numerals for the New Year](/blog/hanoi/tet-decorations-display.jpg)

**Cà phê Giảng** is the mandatory stop, the café that invented **egg coffee**,
served in a tiny cup of whipped yolk and condensed milk over strong Vietnamese
coffee. It sounds wrong and it is excellent.

![A glass of egg coffee on a patterned café table overlooking a busy Old Quarter street](/blog/hanoi/egg-coffee-cafe.jpg)

**Đồng Xuân Market**, the largest covered market in the city, is three floors of
everything, and best at ground level for the food.

Temples next: **Bạch Mã**, the oldest in the Old Quarter, with a carved dragon
relief still tended with fresh flowers, and **Ngọc Sơn**, on its own island in
**Hoàn Kiếm Lake**, reached by a red wooden bridge.

![A carved stone relief of a dragon above a small shrine, with fresh flowers left on the altar](/blog/hanoi/dragon-relief-shrine.jpg)

![The stone gate leading into Ngoc Son Temple, engraved with Chinese characters](/blog/hanoi/ngoc-son-temple-gate.jpg)

![The Đài Nghiên "ink slab" tower at the Ngoc Son Temple complex, framed by banana leaves](/blog/hanoi/dai-nghien-ink-tower.jpg)

![A carved relief of a koi fish leaping through waves among lotus flowers, on a temple wall](/blog/hanoi/koi-fish-relief.jpg)

The lake is the centre of the city and worth a full lap, especially lit up at
night around the Tết new year.

![A lakeside pavilion at Hoan Kiem Lake lit up at night, framed by two large trees](/blog/hanoi/hoan-kiem-lake-at-night.jpg)

Further out, the **Temple of Literature**, Vietnam's first university, built in
1070, and the calmest place in Hanoi.

![The Temple of Literature's courtyard and reflecting pool, lined with red flowers](/blog/hanoi/temple-of-literature-pond.jpg)

Then **Hanoi train street**, the residential alley so narrow that people pull in
their chairs when the train scrapes past. Access comes and goes depending on the
authorities; the cafés along it will tell you the timetable.

![A train's headlights approaching down the narrow, lantern-lit train street at night](/blog/hanoi/train-street-at-night.jpg)

![Tourists sitting along the train street tracks under strings of lights and red flags](/blog/hanoi/train-street-flags.jpg)

The evening was the **Thăng Long Water Puppet Theatre**, puppets performed waist
deep in a pool of water by puppeteers hidden behind a screen, a thousand-year-old
tradition from the Red River delta, and an hour well spent.

## Day 2, the French Quarter

The other Hanoi: wide boulevards, plane trees and colonial facades. **St Joseph's
Cathedral**, blackened neo-gothic that looks transplanted from France, and the
**Opera House**, modelled on the Palais Garnier.

![St Joseph's Cathedral's dark twin towers backlit by the sun, with a statue and topiary in front](/blog/hanoi/st-josephs-cathedral.jpg)

Then the political quarter: the **Presidential Palace** in its mustard yellow,
**Hồ Chí Minh's stilt house** in the gardens behind it, he refused to live in the
palace, so he had a wooden house built beside it, and the **Hồ Chí Minh
Mausoleum**. The mausoleum has short morning hours, a strict dress code and a long
queue; check that it's open before planning the day around it.

![The grey granite Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum with a guard standing at attention out front](/blog/hanoi/ho-chi-minh-mausoleum.jpg)

## Day 3, incense and the citadel

A morning at the **incense village** of Quảng Phú Cầu, an hour south of the city,
where bundles of red incense sticks are fanned out to dry in courtyards. It is as
photogenic as it looks, and it is a working village, ask before pointing a camera
at people.

![A person crouched down among fanned-out bundles of red incense sticks drying in the sun](/blog/hanoi/incense-village.jpg)

The afternoon was the **Thăng Long Imperial Citadel**, the seat of Vietnamese power
for almost eight centuries and a UNESCO site, with a military command bunker from
the American war preserved underneath it.

![The main gate of the Thăng Long Imperial Citadel, with flowerbeds in front](/blog/hanoi/cover.jpg)

![The citadel's stone gate and central watchtower, marked with the New Year](/blog/hanoi/thang-long-citadel-gate.jpg)

## Day 4, Ninh Binh

The day trip everyone tells you to take, correctly. **Ninh Bình**, "Ha Long Bay
on land", is two hours south: limestone karsts rising straight out of rice
paddies.

![A river winding between steep, jungle-covered limestone karsts under a grey sky](/blog/hanoi/ninh-binh-river-gorge.jpg)

We rented bikes to get between stops, and stopped at the old walled gate of
**Hoa Lư**, Vietnam's ancient tenth-century capital, tucked at the foot of the
same karsts.

![The stone gate of Hoa Lu ancient capital, with karst mountains rising behind it](/blog/hanoi/hoa-lu-ancient-capital-gate.jpg)

![Two bicycles parked by a wide river, with a wall of limestone karsts on the far bank](/blog/hanoi/bicycles-by-the-river.jpg)

A rowing boat took us through caves at water level at Tam Cốc, low enough that
the rock brushed the top of our heads.

![A rowing boat carrying passengers in life jackets emerging from a cave onto the river, karsts beyond](/blog/hanoi/boat-through-the-cave.jpg)

Then the viewpoint at **Hang Múa**, reached by about 500 steps, guarded by a
goat that had clearly done this before.

![A white goat standing on a stone wall next to a Vietnamese flag, overlooking the valley below](/blog/hanoi/goat-at-hang-mua.jpg)

![The Hang Mua viewpoint pagoda on its rocky peak, rice paddies and the Ninh Binh valley spread out below](/blog/hanoi/hang-mua-viewpoint.jpg)

Long day, early start, the best photographs of the trip.

## Day 5, the last two stops

**Hỏa Lò Prison**, the "Hanoi Hilton", used by the French for Vietnamese political
prisoners and later for American pilots. The museum is heavily one-sided and still
very much worth seeing. Then **Trấn Quốc Pagoda**, the oldest Buddhist temple in
Hanoi, on a small peninsula in West Lake, a red stupa tower over the water, and a
quiet place to end.

![Tran Quoc Pagoda's red brick tower rising above the trees on its peninsula in West Lake](/blog/hanoi/tran-quoc-pagoda.jpg)

## Practical notes

Crossing the street is the skill to learn: walk slowly, predictably, and don't
stop, the scooters will go around you. Grab is cheap for the further sights.
And leave a full day for Ninh Bình rather than squeezing it into a half.
