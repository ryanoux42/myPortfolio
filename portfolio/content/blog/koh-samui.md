Koh Samui in August 2025, over a long weekend. It took twelve hours to get
there and about ten minutes to stop caring about the twelve hours.

## Friday, getting there

Seven hours of bus, four hours of ferry, and one more hour hunting for the
apartment once I landed. That's the honest version of "flying is expensive".

![A blurry sunset shot through a bus window, shops and wet road passing by](/blog/koh-samui/sunset-from-the-bus.jpg)

The ferry is the good part: open deck, islands passing, and a lot of time to do
nothing.

![A coastal promenade with a small island offshore and a palm tree leaning out over the water](/blog/koh-samui/coastal-view-with-palm-tree.jpg)

First stop was the small temple right by the ferry arrival, a naga staircase
guarded by two golden cobras leading up to a white chedi, and a gentle way to
stretch after half a day sitting down.

![A white and gold naga staircase flanked by two cobra statues, leading up to a temple chedi](/blog/koh-samui/naga-staircase-temple.jpg)

Then **Chaweng Beach Road**, the island's busiest strip, its side streets strung
with rows of coloured paper lanterns overhead. Bars, restaurants, massage shops,
scooters everywhere. Not the reason you come to Samui, but a good place to eat
on the first night.

![Rows of coloured paper lanterns lit up at night above a street in Chaweng](/blog/koh-samui/chaweng-hanging-lanterns.jpg)

## Saturday, temples, then a party

The temple day, run more or less clockwise around the south of the island, past
long stretches of pineapple fields between stops.

![A dirt road running between pineapple fields toward green hills](/blog/koh-samui/pineapple-fields-road.jpg)

- **Wat Rat Chathammaram** (the Red Temple), all deep red and gold, and almost
  empty when I got there.
- **Grandfather and Grandmother Rocks** (Hin Ta Hin Yai), two rock formations
  with a very literal nickname and a very good coastline around them.
- **Guan Yu Shrine**, an enormous red statue of the Chinese general, visible long
  before you arrive.

![The giant green and gold Guan Yu statue towering over its shrine, palm trees on either side](/blog/koh-samui/guan-yu-shrine.jpg)

- **Laem Sor Pagoda** and the **Chedi Laem So** next to it, golden, right on the
  water, and the quietest place I found on the island.
- **Magic Garden** (Secret Buddha Garden), up in the hills: stone figures spread
  through the jungle around a stream. The road up is rough; the view down over
  Samui is worth it.
- **Wat Plai Laem**, with its white eighteen-armed Guan Yin standing in a lake
  full of fish.

![Wat Plai Laem's white eighteen-armed Guan Yin statue rising above its temple hall, both reflected in the lake](/blog/koh-samui/wat-plai-laem.jpg)

- **Wat Phra Yai**, the Big Buddha, twelve golden metres of it facing the sea,
  the landmark you see from the plane.

The evening was the **full moon party**: fire skipping ropes, fire limbo, body
paint under blacklight murals, and a beach packed end to end.

![A wall covered in glowing blacklight paintings of dragons, flowers and a "Full Moon Party" sign](/blog/koh-samui/full-moon-party-blacklight-wall.jpg)

![A scaffolding sign spelling out "Welcome to Thailand" in sparklers on the beach at night](/blog/koh-samui/welcome-to-thailand-beach-sign.jpg)

It's exactly as advertised, a lot, loud, and fun for one night. Getting back by
**speedboat** in the dark was, somehow, the best part of the day.

## Sunday, a day on the water

The whole day at sea, and the reason to come.

![A longtail boat moored off a small granite island, clear turquoise water in the foreground](/blog/koh-samui/cover.jpg)

Snorkelling first, the water is clear enough that the fish come to you.

![A dense school of small fish in clear turquoise water next to a moored boat](/blog/koh-samui/fish-schooling-by-the-boat.jpg)

Then lunch on **Koh Tan**, a tiny island with almost nothing on it, which is the
appeal.

![A small forested island with granite boulders and a longtail boat anchored offshore](/blog/koh-samui/koh-tan-from-the-water.jpg)

After that **Koh Madsum**, better known as **Pig Island**, where pigs genuinely
wander down onto the sand and into the shallows.

The day ended back on land at **Fisherman's Village** in Bophut: old wooden
shophouses, a walking street, and a beach to finish the evening on. The calmest
part of Samui I saw.

## Monday, two last stops

Before leaving: the **Overlap Stone**, a boulder balanced on a hill with a view
across the whole south of the island.

![A large boulder balanced on top of a rock outcrop, with a view down over the coast and sea behind](/blog/koh-samui/overlap-stone.jpg)

And the **Dusit Dheva Cultural Centre**, an odd, ornate complex mixing Thai,
Khmer and Indian architecture. A strange last stop, and a good one.

![The sun setting over the sea, seen through silhouetted trees from a hillside](/blog/koh-samui/sunset-over-the-sea.jpg)

## Worth knowing

Rent a scooter, the sights are spread around the ring road and taxis add up
fast. Do the boat day; skip a temple if you have to, not that. And if you're
arriving by bus and ferry, book the night before and treat the travel day as part
of the trip rather than a cost of it.
