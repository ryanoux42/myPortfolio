Kuala Lumpur, five days in January. It's a city that changes character every few
streets — Chinese temples, Indian markets, colonial squares and a skyline of glass
towers, all within walking distance of each other. I stayed at Expressionz
Professional Suites, close enough to the centre to walk most of it.

## Tuesday — temples and towers

I started away from the centre at the **Thean Hou Temple**, a six-tiered Chinese
temple on a hill with hundreds of red lanterns strung across its courtyard, and one
of the best views back over the city.

Then the two towers. **KL Tower** first, which is the one people skip and
shouldn't: because it stands on a hill, its observation deck is higher than the
Petronas skybridge, and it's the only place you can photograph the **Petronas Twin
Towers** in full.

![The Kuala Lumpur skyline seen from KL Tower, with the Petronas Twin Towers in the middle](/blog/kuala-lumpur/cover.jpg)

Down at ground level, **KLCC Park** sits at the foot of the towers — a good place
to wait for the light to change — and the **Saloma Link**, the golden pedestrian
bridge shaped like a betel leaf, is ten minutes away and best after dark when it
lights up.

The evening covered the older quarters: **Little India** in Brickfields, the
**Central Market** for crafts, **Chinatown** and Petaling Street, and the **Sri
Maha Mariamman Temple**, the oldest Hindu temple in the city, whose tower above the
entrance is covered in dozens of painted deities.

## Wednesday — Batu Caves and colonial KL

**Batu Caves** is the morning: 272 rainbow-painted steps up to a limestone cavern
with a Hindu temple inside it, a 42-metre gold statue of Murugan standing at the
bottom, and monkeys with no respect for your belongings. Go early — it's hot, and
by ten the queue on the stairs barely moves.

Back in town, **Berjaya Times Square** to escape the heat, then the historic centre
on foot: **Merdeka Square**, where Malaysian independence was declared, and the
**River of Life**, the walkway at the confluence of the two rivers that gave the
city its name — best seen in the evening when the fountains and lights are on.

## Thursday — museums and mosques

A slower, indoors day. The **National Museum** for the country's history from the
Malacca sultanate to independence, and the **National Textile Museum** next to
Merdeka Square, which is worth it as much for the Mughal-style building as for the
collection.

Then two mosques. **Masjid Negara**, the national mosque, with its folded blue
umbrella roof and a huge reflecting pool, and **Masjid Jamek**, the oldest in the
city, sitting right where the rivers meet. Both lend robes at the entrance and both
have visiting hours outside prayer times — check before you go.

## Friday — Malacca day trip

A day trip south to **Malacca**: Dutch colonial red buildings around the main
square, the ruins of St. Paul's Church on the hill, the A Famosa gate, Jonker Street
and a river running through the middle of it. Two hours each way by bus, and enough
in one day.

## Practical notes

The metro and monorail cover most of what you'd want, but the city is not built for
walking — footbridges and highways get in the way constantly, so budget more time
than the map suggests. And do KL Tower before the Petronas skybridge, not after.
