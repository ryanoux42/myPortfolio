Eight days is a long time to give one small island, and it changed how the trip
worked. Instead of a list to get through, most days had one thing on them and a
lot of nothing around it: the beach, the pool, and whatever we felt like.

## The water

The best morning was a boat out to snorkel. Sea turtles here are used enough to
people that they carry on grazing while you float above them, and this one came
up for air right underneath me.

![A green sea turtle swimming over pale sand and coral, surrounded by small dark fish](/blog/panglao-island/cover.jpg)

The reef around the boat was worth as much time as the turtles: anemones thick
enough to lose a fish in, and a maroon clownfish that held its ground instead of
hiding.

![A maroon clownfish with pale stripes resting in a sea anemone next to a coral outcrop](/blog/panglao-island/clownfish-in-anemone.jpg)

![A dense sea anemone with pale tentacles, small striped fish sheltering around its edges](/blog/panglao-island/anemone-reef-closeup.jpg)

![The white bow of an outrigger boat cutting through clear blue water, with other boats in the distance](/blog/panglao-island/boat-bow-on-blue-water.jpg)

We went out for dolphins on another trip, which is a very early start and much
more of a gamble: they show up or they don't, and you're watching from the boat
rather than swimming.

![An overcast dawn sky reflected in calm sea water, with rocks in the foreground](/blog/panglao-island/overcast-dawn-seascape.jpg)

![An outrigger boat named "JGWEN FAITH" anchored on clear water with people on board scanning for dolphins](/blog/panglao-island/outrigger-boat-snorkelers.jpg)

## Inland Bohol

Panglao is joined to Bohol by a bridge, and the island behind it is where most
of the day trips go.

The **Chocolate Hills** are the famous one: over a thousand near-identical
conical hills spread across the interior, which turn brown in the dry season and
give the place its name. They look artificial from the viewpoint and aren't. We
rented ATVs for an afternoon and rode out toward them on dirt tracks, which was
its own kind of chaotic fun before we'd even reached the main viewpoint.

![A parked ATV on a muddy field with two conical Chocolate Hills rising behind it](/blog/panglao-island/atv-and-chocolate-hills.jpg)

The **tarsier sanctuary** is the opposite kind of attraction: quiet, small, and
rule-bound. Philippine tarsiers are tiny nocturnal primates that stress easily,
so you walk a short path in near silence and find them sleeping against
branches. **Alicia Panoramic Park** filled another day, further east and much
steeper than it looks.

## Cave pool and waterfall

Two of the better stops were the ones that took the most driving. **Cabagnow
Cave Pool** in Anda is a hole in flat ground that opens into clear water well
below the surface, and the only way in is to jump. **Kinahugan Falls** over in
Jagna is a proper waterfall you can swim under.

## The shooting range

Somewhere in the middle of all this we spent an afternoon at a shooting range,
which had nothing to do with anything and remains the most unexpected thing on
the island.

## Doing less

I've done trips where eight days would have meant fifteen destinations. This one
had maybe six, and the gaps between them were the part I'd repeat. Most of those
gaps looked the same: the resort pool by day, the restaurant lit up in reds and
golds after dark, and a couple of loungers under a tree at the water's edge with
nowhere to be.
