This one wasn't really a trip, it was a surprise. Friends of mine were in Seoul
and one of them had a birthday coming up, so I flew up from the south of the
continent for three days in November and said nothing until I was standing there.
The face was worth the flight.

## The surprise

The whole plan was built around one moment, and it worked: they had no idea I was
coming. Everything after that was improvised, which turned out to be the right
way to see a city you only have three days in.

## Gyeongbokgung and around

The one proper piece of sightseeing was **Gyeongbokgung Palace**, the largest of
Seoul's five grand palaces, and the streets around it. It's enormous, courtyard
after courtyard, with the mountains sitting right behind the rooftops, and the
best part is at the back, where a pavilion sits on an island in the middle of a
lake.

![The Hyangwonjeong pavilion on its island at Gyeongbokgung Palace, with mountains behind](/blog/seoul/cover.jpg)

![The main gate of Gyeongbokgung Palace under a cloudy sky, with visitors in rented hanbok crossing the courtyard](/blog/seoul/gyeongbokgung-main-gate.jpg)

![One of Gyeongbokgung's halls seen past a bare winter tree, with mountains rising behind the palace grounds](/blog/seoul/gyeongbokgung-with-mountains.jpg)

Closer up, the Hyangwonjeong pavilion is even better: a small two-storey
pagoda painted red and green, reached by a narrow footbridge across the pond.

![The Hyangwonjeong pavilion and its footbridge seen up close from across the pond](/blog/seoul/hyangwonjeong-pavilion-close.jpg)

**Gyeonghoeru**, the palace's other pavilion, is the opposite scale: a huge
hall on stone columns rising straight out of a second, larger pond, built for
state banquets rather than quiet walks.

![The Gyeonghoeru pavilion at Gyeongbokgung, a large hall on stone pillars reflected in its pond](/blog/seoul/gyeonghoeru-pavilion.jpg)

The **National Palace Museum**, tucked into the grounds, was worth the half
hour it took: a room of white and blue-and-white ceramics, moon jars included,
in front of painted folding screens.

![A museum room at the National Palace Museum, with blue-and-white ceramic jars in front of painted folding screens](/blog/seoul/national-palace-museum-ceramics.jpg)

Walking the neighbourhoods around the palace is half the visit, and
**Bukchon Hanok Village** is the reason: a whole hillside of preserved hanok
houses, small cafés, and people renting hanbok to walk the alleys in.

![A narrow Bukchon alley lined with a traditional tiled hanok wall and a red brick building](/blog/seoul/bukchon-hanok-alley.jpg)

## A temple in the hills

One quiet afternoon took us out of downtown to a temple tucked into the
hills, its main hall marked **Daeungjeon**, the name given to the hall of the
Buddha in any Korean temple, with pine trees and a rocky peak rising behind it.
A wooden-fenced trail led back down through the forest, empty except for us.

![A temple's Daeungjeon hall with a green tiled roof, pine trees and a rocky mountain peak behind it](/blog/seoul/temple-hall-in-the-hills.jpg)

![A wooden-fenced forest trail lined with stones, winter trees overhead and sunlight breaking through](/blog/seoul/forest-trail-in-the-hills.jpg)

## Hongdae

The evenings were **Hongdae**, the student district near Hongik University: street
performers, bars that stay open far too late, and the sort of energy that makes
you forget what time it is. It's where the birthday actually got celebrated.

![A busy Hongdae street at night, lit up with shop signs and lanterns](/blog/seoul/hongdae-street-at-night.jpg)

On the walk back one night we passed a whole underground wall lit up with
digital art, geometric patterns and painted figures cycling across a huge
screen.

![A long underground wall covered in colourful digital art, geometric patterns and painted figures](/blog/seoul/underground-digital-art-wall.jpg)

## On the cold

I came from tropical weather and packed accordingly, which was a mistake. Seoul
in late November is genuinely cold, though it does mean arriving to trees still
holding their last red leaves against a clear sky.

![A maple tree in full red autumn colour against a blue sky, near one of the palace buildings](/blog/seoul/red-maple-at-the-palace.jpg)

One of my friends lent me a jacket for the whole trip and that solved it, but if
you're island-hopping in Southeast Asia and decide to detour north, look at the
forecast first. I didn't.

## Worth it

Three days is nothing for a city like Seoul, and I saw almost none of it. That
wasn't the point. Sometimes the trip is just the people at the end of it.
